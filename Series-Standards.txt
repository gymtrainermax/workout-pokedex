# The Original 151: Pokémon Workout Series
### Official Series Standards | Version 1.1

**Series Concept:** Create 151 unique workouts inspired by the original 151 Pokémon, combining professional fitness programming, accurate Pokémon references, and entertaining social media content.

Every workout should feel authentic to its Pokémon while remaining effective, accessible, and enjoyable to perform.

---

## 1. Pokémon Authenticity

**1. Know the Pokémon.**  
Research each Pokémon's Pokédex entries, typing, anatomy, abilities, learnset, personality, and behavior before developing its workout.

**2. Use accurate Pokémon references.**  
Every featured move must legitimately belong to that Pokémon's learnset. Distinguish official moves and abilities from our own creative interpretations.

**3. Make the connection obvious.**  
Whenever possible, viewers should recognize the connection between a Pokémon's move and its corresponding exercise without needing an explanation.

**4. Never force a reference.**  
If an exercise doesn't convincingly represent a move, ability, or physical characteristic, it should be reconsidered or included as an ordinary supporting exercise.

**5. Give every Pokémon its own identity.**  
Each workout should reflect what makes that Pokémon unique. Evolutionary families should demonstrate meaningful progression without repeating the same workout.

**6. Establish a unique four-move core moveset.**  
Every Pokémon must have exactly four official moves that define its signature workout identity.

- Each four-move combination must be unique across all 151 Pokémon, regardless of move order.
- Individual moves may repeat between Pokémon.
- Each move should have a corresponding exercise that reflects its action or purpose.
- The core four will guide promotional content and the Pokémon's signature workout card.
- The four-move structure does not restrict the total number of exercises in the complete workout.
- Maintain a master moveset registry to prevent duplicate combinations.

---

## 2. Fitness Programming

**7. Every workout must be legitimate.**  
Follow established exercise science principles, including appropriate volume, intensity, recovery, progression, and exercise selection.

**8. Every exercise must serve a purpose.**  
Include both Pokémon-inspired signature movements and supporting exercises that contribute to an effective training session.

**9. Use proper exercise terminology.**  
Always identify exercises by their actual fitness names alongside their Pokémon-inspired names.

**10. Take advantage of available gym equipment.**  
Use the full range of equipment available, including free weights, cable machines, battle ropes, sleds, medicine balls, resistance bands, and specialty equipment. Choose equipment based on the exercise's effectiveness and visual connection.

**11. Make every workout scalable.**  
Provide beginner, intermediate, and advanced modifications when appropriate. Include sets, repetitions, rest intervals, technique cues, and relevant safety considerations.

**12. Allow different training styles.**  
Not every Pokémon needs a full-body circuit. Workouts may focus on strength, power, endurance, hypertrophy, agility, mobility, balance, or other training goals based on the Pokémon's characteristics.

---

## 3. Video and Social Media

**13. Prioritize visual storytelling.**  
Whenever possible, show the Pokémon's move alongside the corresponding exercise. Use transitions, movement matching, and timing to make the relationship immediately recognizable.

**14. Hook viewers immediately.**  
Capture attention within the first three seconds through a Pokémon reference, entertaining introduction, or visually compelling movement.

**15. Feature the four core moves.**  
The four signature moves should form the foundation of each episode's Reel, TikTok, and promotional workout card. Additional exercises may appear when useful.

**16. Maintain recognizable series branding.**  
Use consistent Pokédex numbering, Pokémon typing, exercise labels, workout cards, and visual elements throughout the series.

**17. Balance entertainment and education.**  
Videos should be fun for Pokémon fans while providing credible fitness information. Humor and references should complement the training content.

**18. Encourage natural engagement.**  
Use Pokémon debates, familiar references, questions, and occasional humor to encourage comments, saves, shares, and continued interest in the series. Avoid forced engagement bait.

---

## 4. Voice and Creative Direction

**19. Sound like GymTrainerMax.**  
Content should feel conversational, knowledgeable, nerdy, occasionally campy, and authentic to a personal trainer who genuinely loves Pokémon.

**20. Avoid AI-sounding writing.**  
Eliminate generic motivational language, exaggerated claims, forced metaphors, repetitive phrasing, unnecessary filler, and em dashes. Follow the Avoid AI Writing guidelines.

**21. Keep the series approachable.**  
Viewers should be able to enjoy the content regardless of their fitness experience or Pokémon knowledge. Provide modifications so different ability levels can participate.

**22. Prioritize quality over quantity.**  
Fully develop, review, and approve each Pokémon workout before moving to the next. The series does not require a daily posting schedule.

---

## 5. Standard Episode Deliverables

Each Pokémon must receive:

1. **Pokémon Research Profile:** Pokédex number, typing, learnset, anatomy, behavior, and defining characteristics.
2. **Training Identity:** Primary fitness goal and why it fits the Pokémon.
3. **Unique Four-Move Core Moveset:** Four official moves and their corresponding exercises, checked against the master registry.
4. **Complete Workout:** Warm-up, working sets, repetitions, rest intervals, and cooldown.
5. **Exercise Modifications:** Appropriate progressions, regressions, and coaching cues.
6. **Pokémon-to-Exercise Audit:** Evaluation of each themed movement's accuracy, training purpose, and visual connection.
7. **Reel and TikTok Package:** Hook, script, filming sequence, on-screen text, and ending.
8. **Social Media Package:** Caption, cover concept, call to action, and five targeted hashtags.
9. **Final Quality Check:** Pokémon accuracy, sound programming, visual clarity, moveset uniqueness, and writing quality.

---

## 6. The Three-Test Approval System

Every signature exercise must pass three tests before being finalized.

**The Pokémon Test:** Does the exercise genuinely represent the Pokémon's move, ability, or physical characteristics?

**The Trainer Test:** Is the exercise legitimate, effective, and appropriate for the workout's training objective?

**The Camera Test:** Is the connection recognizable or visually compelling enough to communicate through video?

An exercise that fails the Pokémon or Camera Test can still be included as supporting training if it passes the Trainer Test. It should not be promoted as a signature move without further development.

---

## Guiding Principle

**The Pokémon determines the creative direction. Exercise science determines the programming. The camera determines how we tell the story.**

151 Pokémon. 151 unique core movesets. 151 thoughtfully designed workouts.
