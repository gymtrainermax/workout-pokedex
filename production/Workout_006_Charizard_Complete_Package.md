# Workout 006 | Charizard: Full Blaze
## Evolution-audited complete workout & production package | Creator-approved program • published Review | October 23, 2026

**Status:** Creator-approved workout published as **Review** on October 23, 2026. `publicWorkout: true`; video scripts are drafts, all four filmed Camera Tests are pending, and this episode is not Final.

**Series:** The Original 151 / GymTrainerMax Workout Pokédex. **Identity:** Advanced full-body power, strength, athletic force, and Charizard's new aerial dimension. **Time:** approximately 65–75 minutes when equipment is reasonably available (allow longer during busy gym conditions). **Structure:** 7 exercises, 22 working sets, 4 official signature moves. **Effort:** Power exercises stay fast and crisp; front squat, pull-up and incline press around RPE 7–8 (roughly 2–3 reps in reserve). No failure training on throws, landmine power sets or sleds. **Frequency:** a demanding standalone workout, generally no more than 1–2 times a week with at least ~48 hours before repeating comparable heavy full-body work; adapt to the rest of the week's training.

## 1. Pokémon Research Profile

- **National Pokédex:** #006. **Species:** Flame Pokémon. **Typing:** Fire / Flying (ordinary Charizard is not Dragon type). **Height:** 1.7 m. **Weight:** 90.5 kg. **Abilities:** Blaze; Solar Power (Hidden).
- **Evolution:** Charmander #004 evolves into Charmeleon #005, then into Charizard #006. Powerful wings, heavy tail, claws, persistent tail flame, speed and strong special attacks inform the programming. Charizard's baseline Special Attack (109) and Speed (100) are stronger than its Attack (84); our portrayal is athletic and explosive, not exclusively a heavy brute-force bodybuilder.
- **Four confirmed official learnset moves:** Dragon Claw (Dragon, Physical; Scarlet/Violet level-up at 1 or TM078); Seismic Toss (Fighting, Physical; FireRed/LeafGreen tutor, Let's Go TM15, **not in the Scarlet/Violet Charizard learnset**); Flare Blitz (Fire, Physical; Scarlet/Violet level-up 62 or TM165); Blast Burn (Fire, Special; Scarlet/Violet TM153).
- **Move mechanics:** Seismic Toss inflicts damage equal to the user's level in core game mechanics, not damage based on how high an enemy is lifted. Flare Blitz carries in-game recoil, which must never be imitated through deliberate self-injury. Blast Burn requires a recharge turn after use; we creatively acknowledge this by prescribing generous recovery, not by claiming a game turn equals a training rest interval.

**Audit decision (October 23, 2026):** The original overhead slam and low-to-high cable lift failed the *continuity* part of our evolution audit, even though they were valid exercises. Charizard now projects a substantially more powerful forward/upward press instead of slamming fire into the floor, and the claw keeps the inherited descending diagonal while adding unilateral step-and-pivot control. These are creative storytelling progressions, not claims that Pokémon must learn the moves in that sequence. Flamethrower remains Charmeleon's featured move and can appear in the evolution recap, not as a fifth core move in Charizard's workout. Charizard has no official Flying-type signature move in this four, but wing footage and pull-up back work carry the aerial identity without falsely labeling an exercise as Fly.

### Evolution-chain acceptance criteria (required before publishing #006)

1. **Scratch → Slash → Dragon Claw:** Keep a recognizable descending diagonal across all stages. Charizard adds *single-arm trunk control plus coordinated stepping/pivoting* rather than merely reversing direction or increasing load. Check the final gesture side by side with prior workout diagrams/clips.
2. **Flame Charge → Flare Blitz:** Preserve forward acceleration. The stage-two trap-bar deadlift and reverse lunge train the force that allows a sled push to be the harder, loaded version. Test one uninterrupted 12–15 m sled drive, not a grinding stall.
3. **Ember → Flamethrower → Blast Burn:** Preserve forward flame projection from the chest. Charizard adds strong leg drive and larger full-body extension, not an unrelated downward slam. Use one forward/upward VFX blast at the end of the real press.
4. **Seismic Toss (new dimension):** Do not invent a predecessor official move. It is Charizard's first aerial-grappling signature. Its real lifting foundation traces through #004 dumbbell RDL and #005 trap-bar deadlift; camera flight is a story effect only, never an actual jump with a sandbag.

**Before final filmed approval:** Confirm each of the three inherited move chains *looks like an escalation* without VFX. If viewers cannot identify the progression, keep final video approval pending and revise the exercise/angle. This is an additional editorial gate, not a passed final filmed Camera Test.

**Signature exercise watch-outs:** A single-arm step-and-pivot cable chop is a coordination progression, not permission to yank heavier weights than Charmeleon. Film an unloaded 3/4-angle test and keep the step only if the actual limb path reads as Dragon Claw without an uncontrolled twist; use the planted single-arm version otherwise. Seismic Toss likewise must visually read as a controlled toss rather than a sandbag dropped without purpose. Inaccessible gyms should swap to controlled sandbag shouldering and change the scene/edit accordingly rather than pretending the substitute is a true toss.

## 4. Full seven-exercise session: 22 working sets

**Warm-up, 9–12 min (not counted as working sets):**
1. 2–3 min easy row, bike or brisk walk.
2. 8 bodyweight squats; 8 hip hinges; 6 alternating thoracic rotations/side; 8 scapular pull-ups or band pull-aparts.
3. 2 x 3 dowel or lightly loaded two-handed landmine squat-to-press rehearsals with a secured attachment; practice a clear bar path and controlled reset.
4. Two light sandbag practice pickups/shoulderings before throwing; 2 light high-to-low single-arm cable chops/side with slow step-and-pivot practice; one unloaded sled rehearsal.
5. Before work sets on front squat, 2–3 progressive warm-up sets at comfortable light-to-moderate weight. These are *not* part of the 22 working sets.

| # | Exercise and role | Sets x reps | Rest after set | Working intensity / purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blast Burn**: Two-Handed Landmine Thruster (signature) | **3 x 5** | **90–120 sec** | Secure landmine, light/moderate load, smooth fast squat-to-press; stop when drive slows |
| 2 | **Seismic Toss**: Sandbag Over-the-Shoulder Toss (signature) | **3 x 4 total** (2/shoulder) | **120–150 sec** | Manageable soft bag, no missed reps or uncontrolled drops |
| 3 | Barbell Front Squat (supporting) | **3 x 5** | **150–180 sec** | RPE 7–8; knee-dominant compound lower-body strength |
| 4 | **Flare Blitz**: Sled Push (signature) | **4 x 12–15 m** | **120 sec** | Moderate-heavy resistance with steady forward drive; do not grind to a halt |
| 5 | Pull-Up or Assisted Pull-Up (supporting) | **3 x 6–8** | **90–120 sec** | Controlled full range; lat and upper-back strength suggesting powerful wings, **not an official Flying move** |
| 6 | Incline Dumbbell Bench Press (supporting) | **3 x 8** | **90–120 sec** | RPE 7–8; controlled upper-body push, distinct from light Svend press |
| 7 | **Dragon Claw**: Single-Arm High-to-Low Cable Chop with Controlled Step-and-Pivot (signature) | **3 x 8/side** | **75 sec after both sides** | Light/moderate load; downward claw path, balanced footwork and controlled return |

**Cooldown, 3–5 min:** Easy walk, quiet nasal or natural breathing, comfortable shoulder/lat, quadriceps/hip, and thoracic mobility. No forced end-range stretching.

**Suggested time budget (approximate, includes transitions):** warm-up 9–12; landmine and sandbag power blocks 12–16; front squat and setup 10–12; sled 9–12; upper strength 12–15; cable chop 6–8; cooldown 3–5. Total roughly 65–75 minutes depending on logistics. Equipment queues can extend this estimate.

**Weekly use / load progression:** This is a demanding advanced session. Use one session initially within a balanced weekly plan. Give adequate recovery before repeating heavy lower-body or overhead work. Over 3–4 weeks, first make reps consistently crisp, then add the smallest appropriate load increase to the front squat, press or sled. Never progress landmine power sets or sandbag tosses at the expense of speed, control, or a safe landing zone. One lighter deload week may be appropriate in ongoing programming. Pain is a reason to modify or stop, not evidence that the fictional recoil effect worked.

## 5. Technique, safety, and equipment

**Blast Burn / two-handed landmine thruster:** Fully secure the landmine anchor and ensure the bar sleeve, weight plates and intended upward/forward press path clear the face and other people. Hold the end with both hands at upper-chest height, use a comfortable squat depth, then stand assertively and press **forward and upward** as the knees and hips extend. Keep torso braced and feet grounded; lower under control and reset every rep. Start with an empty or appropriately light bar and avoid fast, fatiguing high-rep sets. This is a squat-to-press, not a maximal overhead Olympic lift. Beginner alternative: lighter landmine strict two-hand press without the squat; if no landmine is securely available, substitute a light two-hand cable press and label it honestly.

**Seismic Toss / sandbag over-shoulder:** Use a securely filled, soft strongman bag and a gym-approved drop zone with shock-appropriate flooring. From a stable hip-hinge/squat pickup, lap it if needed, extend hips and knees, roll it up to the shoulder, and send it past the shoulder into a **pre-cleared** landing area. Turn *after* the bag lands; don't twist forcefully with load. Alternate the shoulder each rep; four reps are total, not per shoulder. Never perform with people in the bag's arc. If the space doesn't permit throws, substitute sandbag shouldering (3 x 4 total) with a controlled return; label the actual exercise accurately in video.

**Front squat:** Safe rack with J-hooks and spotter/safeties if appropriate. Elbows forward, torso tall, full foot pressure, depth within control. No max attempts. Alternative: front-loaded goblet squat.

**Flare Blitz / sled push:** Clear 12–15 m lane, secure plates, neutral trunk, ribs and pelvis aligned, short driving steps. Start moderate and add only enough weight to slow you a little without losing continuous motion. No real fire, sharp turns, or contact. In games Flare Blitz causes recoil damage; **gym training does not imitate harm**.

**Pull-up:** Full stable grip, controlled start and descent, no kicking or kipping required. Assistance band or machine if needed. Don't force painful overhead position.

**Incline DB press:** Bench at a modest angle (about 15–30 degrees), shoulder blades stable, elbows move within comfortable path, no bouncing or lockout snap. DB weight challenging but manageable.

**Dragon Claw / one-arm step-and-pivot high-to-low cable chop:** Set pulley high. With a secure D-handle grip and room to move, start side-on in a stable athletic stance, hand above and outside the working-side shoulder. Chop diagonally toward the opposite hip while feet and hips pivot together; a small controlled step is **optional** only when balance and coordination are solid. Keep a steady ribcage and avoid whipping the lumbar spine or tugging aggressively on the cable. Return slowly. This is deliberate rotational-resistance training, not a violent weighted slash. The working hand retains the descending direction established in Scratch and Slash. A planted-foot single-arm cable chop is the safer fallback and can be filmed identically.

**Equipment:** Secure landmine station, bar sleeve and suitable plates with a clear press path; soft strongman sandbag and approved dropping platform; squat rack/bar/plates and safety arms; loadable sled and 12–15 m lane; pull-up station or assisted station; adjustable bench and dumbbells; high cable pulley station. No actual flames, smoke, powdered effects, heat, combustion, fire props, or human throwing. VFX only.
