# Workout #019 — Rattata: Route One Scavenger
**STATUS: Creator-approved exercise program and A/B scripts, published in Review October 27, 2026. Not filmed. Four filmed Camera Tests and A/B video tests pending.**

## Pokémon research and training identity
- **Species:** #019 Rattata — original Kanto Normal form. Mouse Pokémon; original Kanto Normal type. Small, swift, adaptable, sharp incisors and a long tail; evolves into Raticate at level 20.
- **Evolution:** Rattata → Raticate at level 20. Alolan regional forms are a separate project subject, not substituted here.
- **Goal:** Learn to move quickly without sprinting recklessly, build braced forward leg drive, practice a controlled tail sweep, and develop a useful clamp/grip.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner+ · **Intensity:** RPE 6–7 on strength work and comfortable control in speed/technical movements.
- **Estimated duration:** 40–50 minutes, including setup and rests.
- **Equipment:** Clear 10 m acceleration lane and marked slowdown; light sled and safe lane; two rubber-coated grip plates; rated low band anchor and resistance band; adjustable dumbbells, bench, goblet weight.

## Four signature official moves and same-name audit
**Legality:** Generation VII Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon level-up: Tackle (Lv1), Tail Whip (Lv1), Quick Attack (Lv4), Bite (Lv10). All four are legal as a regular Kanto Rattata. Special regional Alolan forms are excluded.

| Move | Type | Real exercise | Prescription | Camera / trainer rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quick Attack | Normal | **Short Acceleration Run from Split Stance** | 3 × 8 m, rest 75–90 sec | Same physical acceleration family as Pidgey Quick Attack. Small forward burst followed by smooth stop; no max sprint. |
| Tackle | Normal | **Light Sled Push** | 3 × 10 m, rest 90 sec | Identical safe horizontal push exercise family as Bulbasaur, Squirtle, Caterpie and Pidgey. Small, deliberate steps. |
| Bite | Dark | **Rubber-Coated Plate Pinch Hold** | 2 × 20 sec, rest 60 sec | Controlled two-hand plate pinch/clamp represents firm incisors; no jaw exercise or real biting. |
| Tail Whip | Normal | **Standing Single-Arm Resistance-Band Hip-Level Sweep** | 2 × 8/side, rest 60–75 sec | Light horizontal sweeping band motion with small synchronized hip and foot turn; do not twist just the low back. |

**Uniqueness:** Both four-move unordered cores are distinct from the 18 published #001–018 combinations and from each other. Existing shared move names retain their published exercise families.

## Complete seven-exercise workout
| Order | Move | Real exercise | Working prescription | Rest | Coaching cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quick Attack | **Short Acceleration Run from Split Stance** | 3 × 8 m | 75–90 sec | Begin from a split stance and run 8 m under control; decelerate in a marked lane. |
| 2 | Tackle | **Light Sled Push** | 3 × 10 m | 90 sec | Brace ribs and drive short even steps; clear the entire sled lane. |
| 3 | Bite | **Rubber-Coated Plate Pinch Hold** | 2 × 20 sec | 60 sec | Grip a small rubber plate vertically near thighs, shoulders relaxed; put down before grip slips. |
| 4 | Tail Whip | **Standing Single-Arm Resistance-Band Hip-Level Sweep** | 2 × 8/side | 60–75 sec | Band anchored securely at waist/hip height; sweep at hip level and pivot feet naturally. |
| 5 | Supporting | **Goblet Squat** | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Full foot on ground; comfortable depth and ribs stacked. |
| 6 | Supporting | **Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row** | 2 × 10 | 75 sec | Chest stays on bench; elbows row smoothly and controlled release. |
| 7 | Supporting | **Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift** | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Soft knees, push hips back, keep dumbbells close and range comfortable. |

**Total:** 16 working sets; warm-ups and cooldowns excluded. Work in actual program order rather than reel-montage order. The two Pokémon workouts are *alternative episodes*, not back-to-back required sessions.

### Warm-up
7–8 minutes: walk or cycle 2 minutes; 8 easy squats, 8 unloaded hinges, shoulder mobility, 2 × 5 m walk-through accelerations, 1 light sled pass, easy band sweeps. Warm-up is not working volume.

### Cooldown
3–5 minutes easy walking, relaxed forearm and hip mobility, and calm breathing. No maximal grip burnouts.

## Beginner / intermediate / advanced modifications
- **Beginner:** Walk 5–8 m instead of running; empty or very light sled; soft sandbag squeeze if plates are difficult; use a lighter band; supported squat/hinge as needed.
- **Intermediate:** Extend accelerations to 10 m if there is room to stop and technique stays controlled; increase sled/plate load one small increment; keep full rest.
- **Advanced:** Keep this as a technique-focused lower-intensity day; do not add risky high-speed cuts or maximal grip attempts.

## Safety and camera readiness
No collisions or sudden cuts. Allow more space to decelerate than to accelerate. Keep shoulders stacked while pressing sled, rated band anchor fixed, and feet free to pivot during tail sweep. Only grip rubber-coated plates light enough to control, keep near floor, and stop if slipping. No real biting, animals, projectiles or injury VFX.

## Cross-evolution continuity
- **Tackle:** Rattata uses the same **sled push** as Bulbasaur, Pidgey, Caterpie and related workouts; Raticate retains the same Tackle exercise family with more load. Squirtle's march remains a light sled variation.
- **Quick Attack:** Rattata's **Short Acceleration Run from Split Stance** matches Pidgey's published Quick Attack, not a different sprint/crawl exercise.
- **Bite → Hyper Fang:** Safe rubber-coated plate pinch hold develops into a loaded plate pinch carry; both show a recognizable clamping grip, never actual biting.
- **Tail Whip → Iron Tail:** Light hip-level band sweep grows into a controlled cable sweep with feet/hip pivot, retaining the horizontal path. Iron Tail is a Gen VII move tutor, not an ordinary level-up move.
- **Quick Attack → Sucker Punch:** Ground movement develops into reaction-triggered cable striking for a new move. They are *different* official moves and not represented as canonical upgrades.
