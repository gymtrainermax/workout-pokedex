# Workout #020 — Raticate: Fang Force
**STATUS: Creator-approved exercise program and A/B scripts, published in Review October 27, 2026. Not filmed. Four filmed Camera Tests and A/B video tests pending.**

## Pokémon research and training identity
- **Species:** #020 Raticate — original Kanto Normal form. Mouse Pokémon; original Kanto Normal type. Rattata evolves into Raticate at level 20. Larger incisors and stronger physical attack, with long whiskers and active territorial behavior.
- **Evolution:** Rattata → Raticate at level 20. Alolan regional forms are a separate project subject, not substituted here.
- **Goal:** Amplify Rattata’s sled drive and pinching grip into heavier transport, upgrade the tail sweep under cable resistance, and add a precise reactive strike with balanced full-body strength.
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate+ · **Intensity:** RPE 7–8 on strength work and comfortable control in speed/technical movements.
- **Estimated duration:** 55–65 minutes, including setup and rests.
- **Equipment:** Clear sled lane and rated sled; rubber-coated training plate(s) and unobstructed carry path; two cable handles with light cable resistance; trap bar and plates; chest-supported bench and dumbbells; incline bench and dumbbells.

## Four signature official moves and same-name audit
**Legality:** Generation VII Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon: Tackle (Lv1); Hyper Fang (Lv16 before or after evolution, reserve it creatively for Raticate); Sucker Punch (Lv29 as Raticate); Iron Tail (US/UM move tutor). Rattata evolves into regular Raticate at Lv20. This is NOT Alolan Raticate.

| Move | Type | Real exercise | Prescription | Camera / trainer rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tackle | Normal | **Moderately Loaded Sled Push** | 4 × 12 m, rest 120 sec | The exact same sled-push family as Rattata and earlier Tackle workouts; increased resistance and distance, not a body collision. |
| Hyper Fang | Normal | **Rubber-Coated Plate Pinch Carry** | 3 × 15 m, rest 90 sec | Rattata Bite plate pinch grows into a careful short loaded carry. The jaw analogy is a grip action; no actual biting. |
| Iron Tail | Steel | **Standing Single-Arm Cable Hip-Level Sweep with Pivot** | 3 × 8/side, rest 90 sec | A loaded version of Rattata Tail Whip’s hip-height sweeping path; keep cable light and turn feet and hips together. |
| Sucker Punch | Dark | **Split-Stance Single-Arm Cable Punch on Cue** | 3 × 6/side, rest 90 sec | Short planted step and reactive cable press after a cue. Pokémon move has priority only when target attacks; no literal contact. |

**Uniqueness:** Both four-move unordered cores are distinct from the 18 published #001–018 combinations and from each other. Existing shared move names retain their published exercise families.

## Complete seven-exercise workout
| Order | Move | Real exercise | Working prescription | Rest | Coaching cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sucker Punch | **Split-Stance Single-Arm Cable Punch on Cue** | 3 × 6/side | 90 sec | Use very light cable weight, short controlled straight punch on a simple cue; no elbow snap. |
| 2 | Tackle | **Moderately Loaded Sled Push** | 4 × 12 m | 120 sec | Brace, take forceful short steps, control sled path, stop gradually. |
| 3 | Supporting | **Trap Bar Deadlift** | 3 × 5 | 120–150 sec | Use ramp sets before three working sets at RPE 7–8; no grinding. |
| 4 | Hyper Fang | **Rubber-Coated Plate Pinch Carry** | 3 × 15 m | 90 sec | Rubber plates near thighs, short slow carry on clear floor, set down before fatigue. |
| 5 | Iron Tail | **Standing Single-Arm Cable Hip-Level Sweep with Pivot** | 3 × 8/side | 90 sec | Control diagonal-to-side sweep and turn feet/hips together; no ballistic spinal twist. |
| 6 | Supporting | **Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row** | 3 × 8 | 90 sec | Chest on bench, smooth retraction with complete return. |
| 7 | Supporting | **Incline Dumbbell Bench Press** | 3 × 8 | 90–120 sec | Maintain stable feet and reasonable range, resist bouncing. |

**Total:** 22 working sets; warm-ups and cooldowns excluded. Work in actual program order rather than reel-montage order. The two Pokémon workouts are *alternative episodes*, not back-to-back required sessions.

### Warm-up
9–10 minutes easy cardio, brisk walk, 8 unloaded hinges, 8 bodyweight squats, wrist/shoulder mobility; 1 light sled pass, light cable punch and sweep rehearsals, 2–3 gradually heavier trap-bar warm-up sets (not counted).

### Cooldown
3–5 minutes easy walking, light wrist/forearm mobility and gentle hip mobility; normal breathing and hydration.

## Beginner / intermediate / advanced modifications
- **Beginner:** Reduce to 2 sets per signature move, use an unweighted sled or farmer carry with rubber dumbbells instead of a plate pinch, cable punches at very light load, goblet hinge in place of trap bar.
- **Intermediate:** Perform prescribed RPE 7–8 on safe resistance work; sled and carries remain crisp. Start cable cues slow before progressing speed.
- **Advanced:** Increment sled and trap-bar load only while movement quality remains high; do not sprint with plate carries or twist through the lumbar spine.

## Safety and camera readiness
No hitting people or equipment, no actual bites, no contact stunts. Set down rubber plates before slipping; keep pinch carries close to floor and off other people’s paths. Anchor cables securely, use modest loads for fast punches and hip sweeps. Use full-body turns rather than isolated lumbar rotation. Sled lanes must be clear with a gradual stop; squat/hinge load selection remains submaximal.

## Cross-evolution continuity
- **Tackle:** Rattata uses the same **sled push** as Bulbasaur, Pidgey, Caterpie and related workouts; Raticate retains the same Tackle exercise family with more load. Squirtle's march remains a light sled variation.
- **Quick Attack:** Rattata's **Short Acceleration Run from Split Stance** matches Pidgey's published Quick Attack, not a different sprint/crawl exercise.
- **Bite → Hyper Fang:** Safe rubber-coated plate pinch hold develops into a loaded plate pinch carry; both show a recognizable clamping grip, never actual biting.
- **Tail Whip → Iron Tail:** Light hip-level band sweep grows into a controlled cable sweep with feet/hip pivot, retaining the horizontal path. Iron Tail is a Gen VII move tutor, not an ordinary level-up move.
- **Quick Attack → Sucker Punch:** Ground movement develops into reaction-triggered cable striking for a new move. They are *different* official moves and not represented as canonical upgrades.
