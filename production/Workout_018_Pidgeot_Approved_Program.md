# Workout #018 — Pidgeot: Sky Sovereign
**STATUS: Creator-approved program and A/B script for public Review, October 27, 2026. Filming and four Camera Tests pending; not Final.**

## Pokémon research profile
- **National Pokédex:** #018
- **Type:** Normal / Flying
- **Stage:** Final evolution / full flight power
- **Anatomy and character:** Bird Pokémon; Normal/Flying. Huge powerful wings, extreme flight-speed described in the games, swept-back crest and aerial control. No Mega Evolution powers are assumed for the standard Pidgeot workout.
- **Workout identity:** Deliver strong forward force, coordinated lateral travel, high-intent wind waves and a precise cable wing strike, while preserving sound full-body strength training.
- **Level and intensity:** Advanced; RPE 7–8 on strength lifts; power sets end if speed or form drops
- **Estimated duration:** 65–75 minutes including rests, setup and transitions.
- **Equipment:** Unobstructed 12–15 m sled lane and safely loadable sled; mini loop resistance band; two battle ropes and rated floor anchor; adjustable single cable column; trap bar and plates; incline bench and dumbbells; chest-supported bench.

**Legality reference:** Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon (Generation VII), with inheritance noted for Brave Bird. A chosen move may require training or a move-relearner after the usual evolution level; the gym progression is artistic, not a literal move-learning timeline.

**Moveset:** Exactly four distinct legitimate moves; this four-move combination is unique against published #001–015 and proposed #016–018. Individual moves may repeat as long as their exercise families are preserved.

## Complete seven-exercise workout
**Programming:** Work in this order, regardless of the 70-second reel edit. Working sets do not include ramp sets, warm-up drills, or cooldown. Keep a comfortable reserve and stop speed work when control slips.

| Order | Pokémon move | Real gym exercise | Sets × dose | Rest | Coaching cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brave Bird | Moderately Heavy Sled Push with Acceleration Emphasis | 4 × 12 m | 120–150 sec | Use firm torso, controlled power through floor and gradual stop. No running into targets. |
| 2 | Tailwind | Mini-Band Lateral Shuffle | 3 × 8 steps/side | 75–90 sec | Mini-band above knees, small steps, knee tracks over foot, no rushed knee valgus. |
| 3 | Hurricane | Double-Arm Outside-Circle Battle Rope Power Waves | 3 × 15 sec | 90–120 sec | Large powerful circles with controlled shoulders, brief quality work and full recovery. |
| 4 | Air Slash | Half-Kneeling Single-Arm High-to-Low Cable Sweep | 3 × 8/side | 90 sec | Match Butterfree Air Slash exactly in apparatus and sweep path; smooth half-kneeling torso control. |
| 5 | Supporting | Trap Bar Deadlift | 3 × 5 | 150 sec | Build to controlled RPE 7–8; progressive ramp sets not counted as working sets. |
| 6 | Supporting | Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row | 3 × 8 | 90 sec | Keep chest firmly supported; avoid momentum. |
| 7 | Supporting | Incline Dumbbell Bench Press | 3 × 8 | 90–120 sec | Comfortable range and gradual return, no arching excessively. |

**Session volume:** 22 prescribed sets (all working sets).

### Warm-up
9–12 minutes: easy cardio, hip/ankle mobility, 8 unloaded hinges, light lateral band walks, one unloaded sled pass, low-intensity battle-rope circles, light cable sweeps and 2–3 trap-bar progressive warm-up sets. No warm-up sets count toward 22 working sets.

### Cooldown
3–5 minutes easy walking, gentle shoulder and hip mobility, relaxed breathing.

## Beginner / intermediate / advanced modifications
- **Beginner:** Replace heavier sled drive with a light sled march, omit acceleration intent, use bodyweight side steps, gentle rope waves, a light resistance-band sweep and kettlebell deadlift.
- **Intermediate:** Follow the same movement choices at lower resistance and shorter distances; maintain quality and the full rest prescription. Treat rope power as moderate.
- **Advanced:** Increase just one variable as form permits: sled load OR speed, rope amplitude OR duration, cable tension OR reps. Avoid all-out loaded sprinting.

## Equipment and safety
No actual jumps, diving, collision, bird-like suspended stunts or recoil demonstrations. Sled pushes use a fully clear lane, controlled stops and supportive footwear. Rope anchors and cable carabiners must be rated/checked before each set. Maintain knee alignment in lateral mini-band shuffles. Use a safe half-kneeling setup and keep the sweep path free of other people. Stop for pain, dizziness, loss of breath control or declining power quality.

## Same-name move continuity audit
- **Tackle (Pidgey):** Light sled push. Exactly the established forward sled family already seen with Bulbasaur, Squirtle, Caterpie and Metapod. It is not a crawl or a body collision.
- **Air Slash (Pidgeot):** Half-kneeling single-arm high-to-low cable sweep. Same real exercise and silhouette used for Butterfree’s Air Slash; a different difficulty does not redefine the attack.
- **Gust → Whirlwind → Hurricane:** A planned continuous rope progression: small alternating waves, outward circles, bigger deliberate double-arm circles. These are three *different* game moves, not canonical move upgrades.
- **Quick Attack → Agility → Tailwind:** Short acceleration, reactive footwork and resisted lateral steps. Tailwind is a team-speed status move in the games, not a literal gym performance guarantee.
- **Sand Attack → Wing Attack → Air Slash:** A light diagonal wing/sand sweep develops into a cable-driven wing path, then a precise half-kneeling high-to-low sweep.
- **Tackle → Pidgeotto trap-bar bridge → Brave Bird:** The forward-power theme begins with a sled push, builds hip/leg strength at stage two and culminates in a powerful sled drive. Pidgeotto's deadlift is a *supporting* exercise, not an invented move.
