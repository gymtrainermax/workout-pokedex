# Workout #021 — Spearow: Flock Fighter
**STATUS: Creator-approved workouts and matched A/B scripts published in Review October 28, 2026. Filming and four actual Camera Tests pending.**

## Pokémon research, legality and training goal
- **National Pokédex:** #021 · **Species:** Spearow · **Type:** Normal / Flying.
- **Anatomy and behavior:** Tiny Bird Pokémon, Normal/Flying type. In Ultra Sun, Spearow is described as fearless when protecting its territory; its shorter wings make prolonged flight difficult.
- **Evolution:** Spearow → Fearow at level 20. This is a distinct two-stage family from Pidgey → Pidgeotto → Pidgeot.
- **Workout identity:** An assertive, land-based hunting bird that learns quick reaction footwork, precise forward beak projection, rhythmic multi-hit coordination and deliberate wing sweeps.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner+ · **Target duration:** 40–50 min including warm-up, rest and transitions · **Loaded intensity:** RPE 6–7.
- **Equipment:** Clear marked 8 m acceleration lane plus generous deceleration area; rated secure resistance-band anchor; light band; two light independent cable handles; single cable stack; goblet weight; adjustable dumbbells and chest-supported bench.
- **Official learnset — Gen VII Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon:** Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon level-up: Peck Lv1 (Flying), Pursuit Lv8 (Dark), Fury Attack Lv11 (Normal), Aerial Ace Lv15 (Flying). All four are learned by Spearow without a tutor or egg-move exception.
- **Narrative:** Spearow attacks Ash and Pikachu in anime episode 1; the Spearow reappears evolved as Fearow to oppose Pidgeot in "Pallet Party Panic!" This is anime continuity, not a claim that the featured attacks were shown in those episodes.

## Four official signature moves
| Official move | Type | Real gym exercise | Prescription | Camera / trainer rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pursuit | Dark | **Short Acceleration Run on Cue** | 3 × 8 m, rest 90 sec | Start from a safe standing start on a cue, accelerate a short marked distance, and stop gradually; a controlled chase rather than a collision. |
| Peck | Flying | **Standing Single-Arm Resistance-Band Forward Press** | 3 × 8/side, rest 75 sec | Straight, precise chest-height press: the beak travels forward in a controlled line; same forward-arm path evolves into Drill Peck. |
| Fury Attack | Normal | **Standing Alternating Light Cable Punch** | 2 × 5/side, rest 75 sec | Alternate compact straight cable presses with a measured return; represents repeated small pecks, never contact or elbow snapping. |
| Aerial Ace | Flying | **Standing Single-Arm High-to-Low Cable Sweep** | 2 × 8/side, rest 75–90 sec | Trace one controlled diagonal wing-shaped path with a light cable handle; Fearow retains the same cable-sweep family. |

## Complete seven-exercise workout
| Order | Move / theme | Real gym exercise | Sets × repetitions or distance | Rest | Coaching note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pursuit | **Short Acceleration Run on Cue** | 3 × 8 m | 90 sec | Use one predictable cue; clear the run and stopping zones before each repetition. |
| 2 | Peck | **Standing Single-Arm Resistance-Band Forward Press** | 3 × 8/side | 75 sec | Ribs stacked over hips, steady wrist and a smooth return against secure band tension. |
| 3 | Supporting | **Goblet Squat** | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Whole foot stays grounded; comfortable depth with an upright torso. |
| 4 | Fury Attack | **Standing Alternating Light Cable Punch** | 2 × 5/side | 75 sec | Use two lightweight cables, no impact and no aggressive elbow lockout. |
| 5 | Supporting | **Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift** | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Hinge at hips, keep dumbbells close, finish tall without overextending. |
| 6 | Supporting | **Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row** | 2 × 10 | 75 sec | Keep the torso supported, row elbows back smoothly. |
| 7 | Aerial Ace | **Standing Single-Arm High-to-Low Cable Sweep** | 2 × 8/side | 75–90 sec | Track a smooth downward wing path with light resistance; pivot feet gently. |

**Total:** 16 working sets. Warm-up/cooldown and warm-up ramp sets not counted. Session should be scheduled as a workout in itself; consecutive evolutions need not be trained on the same day.

### Warm-up
7–8 min: 2 minutes easy walking; shoulder circles, 8 air squats, 8 unloaded hip hinges, 2 × 5 m walk-to-jog reactions with generous stopping space, 8 very light band presses and 6 light cable wing sweeps per side. Warm-up work does not count as working sets.

### Cooldown
3–5 min: walk until breathing settles; comfortable wrist, shoulder and hip mobility. No aggressive neck stretches.

## Scaled difficulty
- **Beginner:** Substitute an 8 m brisk walk for Pursuit; reduce band/cable resistance; use a single-arm press with a soft band and no forced elbow lockout; use a supported goblet box squat.
- **Intermediate:** Increase range slightly and use a purposeful auditory cue for Pursuit only if the deceleration lane remains clear; modestly increase presses and rows, keeping reps clean.
- **Advanced:** Use as a light coordination session; do not turn short accelerations into maximal sprinting or rapid cable punches into ballistic elbow snapping.

## Safety
Never strike a human or target with body parts or cable handles. For Pursuit, leave more room to decelerate than to accelerate and avoid abrupt cuts. Secure band anchor and check cable lines. For Fury Attack, use very light cable load and controlled extension; no hyperextension or head-thrust imitation. Let hips and feet pivot during Aerial Ace; do not twist from the lumbar spine.

## Connected evolution and rivalry
Spearow Peck band forward press → Fearow Drill Peck split-stance landmine press (same forward single-arm press family).
Spearow Aerial Ace cable wing sweep → Fearow Aerial Ace cable wing sweep plus deliberate step (same-name rule).
Spearow Pursuit cue acceleration → Fearow Agility lateral shuffle plus acceleration (related reaction/footwork themes, different named moves).
Spearow Fury Attack controlled alternating cable strikes → Fearow Drill Run medicine-ball rotation (different moves; thematic attack escalation, not a direct mechanical chain).
Cross-family check: Fearow Agility exactly matches Pidgeotto Agility's lateral-shuffle-to-acceleration family.
