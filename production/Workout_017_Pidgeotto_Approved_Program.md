# Workout #017 — Pidgeotto: Territory Patrol
**STATUS: Creator-approved program and A/B script for public Review, October 27, 2026. Filming and four Camera Tests pending; not Final.**

## Pokémon research profile
- **National Pokédex:** #017
- **Type:** Normal / Flying
- **Stage:** Middle evolution / control the territory
- **Anatomy and character:** Bird Pokémon; Normal/Flying. Larger wings and stronger talons, vigilant territorial patrols, good eyesight, predator behavior. Evolves from Pidgey at level 18 and into Pidgeot at level 36.
- **Workout identity:** Control directional movement, generate broader rope spirals, cut a stronger wing path, and stabilize a grounded perch.
- **Level and intensity:** Intermediate; RPE 7–8 on strength lifts; shuffles and ropes prioritize quality
- **Estimated duration:** 50–60 minutes including rests, setup and transitions.
- **Equipment:** Clear shuffle/acceleration lane; paired ropes with rated anchor; adjustable cable column; trap bar and weights; adjustable bench and dumbbells; sturdy fixed rail or rack upright for fingertip support.

**Legality reference:** Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon (Generation VII), with inheritance noted for Brave Bird. A chosen move may require training or a move-relearner after the usual evolution level; the gym progression is artistic, not a literal move-learning timeline.

## Complete seven-exercise workout
**Programming:** Work in this order, regardless of the 70-second reel edit. Working sets do not include ramp sets, warm-up drills, or cooldown. Keep a comfortable reserve and stop speed work when control slips.

| Order | Pokémon move | Real gym exercise | Sets × dose | Rest | Coaching cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agility | Lateral Shuffle into Short Acceleration | 3 × 2 × 4 m shuffle + 5 m acceleration | 90 sec | Short low-amplitude shuffle, no forced pivots; clear lane and smooth deceleration. |
| 2 | Whirlwind | Outside-Circle Battle Rope Waves | 3 × 20 sec | 90 sec | Make outward circles with controlled shoulders and a stable lower body. |
| 3 | Wing Attack | Standing Single-Arm High-to-Low Cable Sweep | 3 × 8/side | 75–90 sec | Low-to-moderate load, smooth diagonal downward sweep, pelvis stable. |
| 4 | Supporting | Trap Bar Deadlift | 3 × 5–6 | 120–150 sec | Use progressive warm-up sets first, push floor away, no jerking or rounding. |
| 5 | Supporting | Incline Dumbbell Bench Press | 2 × 8 | 90 sec | Controlled lowering, elbows comfortable below shoulder height. |
| 6 | Supporting | Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row | 2 × 8–10 | 90 sec | Chest supported, no hip drive, full controlled return. |
| 7 | Roost | Supported Single-Leg Balance Hold with Slow Breathing | 2 × 25 sec/side | 45–60 sec | Light touch of a sturdy rack for balance, soft knee, breathe normally; no eyes-closed challenge. |

**Session volume:** 18 prescribed sets (16 strength/athletic training sets plus 2 controlled balance-skill sets; Roost is not counted as a heavy strength lift).

### Warm-up
8–10 minutes: easy rowing or walking, supported side steps, gentle shoulder circles, split-stance holds, one light rope pass, low cable sweeps and two progressive trap-bar warm-up sets. These are not working sets.

### Cooldown
3–5 minutes walking, gentle hip and upper-back mobility, relaxed breathing.

## Beginner / intermediate / advanced modifications
- **Beginner:** Use simple side steps instead of acceleration, short rope circles, light band instead of cable, kettlebell deadlift, floor/bench press and supported two-leg balance.
- **Intermediate:** Maintain the written RPE, take full rest between deadlift sets, perform precise movement and carry over Pidgey’s smaller wind arcs.
- **Advanced:** Slightly raise cable or trap-bar resistance when technique is consistent, keeping side-shuffles short and safe. The Roost balance hold stays calm.

## Equipment and safety
No sharp change of direction at top speed. Mark side-step paths with cones or tape and ensure extra stopping space. Rope anchor and cable clip must be rated and secure. Whirlwind ropes are moderate effort and require shoulder clearance. Roost is a supported balance drill, not injury rehabilitation or recovery treatment. Use a trap bar only if form and equipment are familiar; otherwise substitute an appropriate kettlebell hinge.

## Same-name move continuity audit
- **Tackle (Pidgey):** Light sled push. Exactly the established forward sled family already seen with Bulbasaur, Squirtle, Caterpie and Metapod. It is not a crawl or a body collision.
- **Air Slash (Pidgeot):** Half-kneeling single-arm high-to-low cable sweep. Same real exercise and silhouette used for Butterfree’s Air Slash; a different difficulty does not redefine the attack.
- **Gust → Whirlwind → Hurricane:** A planned continuous rope progression: small alternating waves, outward circles, bigger deliberate double-arm circles. These are three *different* game moves, not canonical move upgrades.
- **Quick Attack → Agility → Tailwind:** Short acceleration, reactive footwork and resisted lateral steps. Tailwind is a team-speed status move in the games, not a literal gym performance guarantee.
- **Sand Attack → Wing Attack → Air Slash:** A light diagonal wing/sand sweep develops into a cable-driven wing path, then a precise half-kneeling high-to-low sweep.
- **Tackle → Pidgeotto trap-bar bridge → Brave Bird:** The forward-power theme begins with a sled push, builds hip/leg strength at stage two and culminates in a powerful sled drive. Pidgeotto's deadlift is a *supporting* exercise, not an invented move.
