# Starter Evolution Continuity Standard
**Effective October 27, 2026 | Creator-approved direction for all future evolution families**

## Connected-workout rule
Plan a complete evolution family before locking the first stage. Choose four legitimate signature moves and seven purposeful exercises per Pokémon, check uniqueness of each four-move combination against all published Pokémon, and make physical movement themes develop across stages. Preserve each species' actual identity and learnsets: thematic progressions are **creative workout relationships**, never claims that Pokémon attacks literally evolve into one another in the games.

## Design checkpoints
1. **Move and theme planning:** Define 3–4 recognizable movement/skill families across all stages, reserving signature finishers for final forms. A move may be a status move or supporting skill. Do not force a false one-to-one three-move chain merely to meet a template.
2. **Exercise continuity:** Preserve at least one visible element of each continuing movement: direction, muscle action, object path, apparatus or stable skill. Change controlled variables such as load, speed, stance, complexity or scale. Different Pokémon may use **the same move** with different training goals; e.g., Bulbasaur's Tackle uses a Sled Push and Squirtle's Tackle uses a Light Sled March.
3. **Training dosage:** Meaningful programming progression, not arbitrary extra volume: first stage teaches skills, middle stage develops loaded control, final stage demonstrates strength/power with adequate recovery and scaling. Respect camera/venue safety over effects.
4. **Filming:** Capture clean effects-free comparisons from consistent angles for all family stages. Their connection should remain legible before VFX. Film four actual Camera Tests for each episode and do not mark filmed QA as passed prematurely.
5. **A/B scripts:** Each episode uses two time-aligned cuts: A = gym humor, B = Pokémon/game humor; visuals, exercises, subtitles except corresponding dialogue, timing, music, graphic overlays, cover, caption and CTA held constant.
6. **Publishing:** Creator approves workouts before public `Review`. Keep Final unavailable until footage, four filmed Camera Tests, and A/B and editorial audit pass. Publish family navigation and an evolution-movement explanation with each episode.

## Kanto Grass line: Bulbasaur → Ivysaur → Venusaur
The original approved moves are **not** literal four-slot move evolutions. Communicate thematic connections honestly:
- Growing plant power: **Seed Bomb** medicine-ball slam → **Growth** dumbbell thruster → **Solar Beam** landmine push press. Small grounded impact → stronger whole-body extension → powerful upper-body projection.
- Vines to overgrowth: **Vine Whip** alternating rope waves → **Grassy Glide** lateral slider lunge → **Frenzy Plant** double rope power slams. Whipping arms, stronger planted footwork, then full-body rope power. Related *plant/ground-control themes*, not one repeated exercise.
- Strength foundation: **Tackle** sled push, then supporting **Trap Bar Deadlift** and **Barbell Back Squat**. Progressive leg force; do not claim later moves are Tackle evolutions.
- Tactical specialization: Ivysaur's **Sludge Bomb** becomes a Poison-themed identity introduced in stage two; Venusaur adds **Poison Powder** and **Giga Drain**. These remain distinct official moves, with no fabricated stage-one Poison move.
- **Final highlight:** Venusaur's Frenzy Plant is the fully evolved Grass-starter ultimate attack.

## Kanto Fire line: Charmander → Charmeleon → Charizard
- **Scratch** single-arm high-to-low cable pulldown → **Slash** two-hand high-to-low cable woodchop → **Dragon Claw** single-arm high-to-low cable chop with controlled step-and-pivot. Preserve the descending claw path while adding hip control and footwork.
- **Flame Charge** unloaded short acceleration → Charmeleon's supporting trap-bar deadlift/lunges → **Flare Blitz** resisted sled push. Move from raw sprint mechanics to heavy loaded propulsion.
- **Ember** medicine-ball chest pass → **Flamethrower** light standing Svend press → **Blast Burn** two-hand landmine thruster. Small forward projectile → sustained forward pressure → leg-driven fire projection.
- **Seismic Toss** remains a new final-evolution anime signature based on earlier hinge/deadlift competence, not an invented move-chain predecessor.
- **Final highlight:** Blast Burn is the Fire-starter ultimate attack.

## Kanto Water line: Squirtle → Wartortle → Blastoise
Four deliberate matching skill families:
- Water projection: **Water Gun** band press → **Water Pulse** half-kneeling single-arm cable press → **Hydro Cannon** standing dual-cable power press.
- Shell defense: **Withdraw** forearm plank → **Protect** tall-kneeling Pallof press → **Iron Defense** double-kettlebell front-rack carry.
- Shell / tail rotation: **Rapid Spin** band trunk rotation → **Aqua Tail** landmine 180s → **Surf** rotational medicine-ball scoop toss to an approved wall.
- Forward charge: **Tackle** light sled march → **Aqua Jet** light acceleration sled push → **Wave Crash** moderately heavy continuous sled push.
- **Final highlight:** Hydro Cannon is the Water-starter ultimate attack.
- **Cross-line Tackle:** Squirtle intentionally marches its light sled, a novice-friendly variation of Bulbasaur's more forceful sled push. Reusing a move and related apparatus is allowed when the purpose and execution are species-specific.

## Kanto finale
Frenzy Plant / Blast Burn / Hydro Cannon form a narrative trio for the fully evolved starter episodes. Show comparisons among all three family pages but keep each Pokémon's routine, difficulty and safety independent.
