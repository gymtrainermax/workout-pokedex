# Early-Route Aerial Rivalry — Spearow → Fearow (#021–022)

**Creator-approved two workout programs and four A/B scripts published as Review October 28, 2026. Filming and Camera Tests are pending.** Rattata/Raticate are a separate completed pair. Both Spearow and Fearow are creator-approved for Review publication; not filmed.

## Pokémon/anime identity
- **Spearow** (#021), Normal/Flying, evolves into **Fearow** (#022) at **level 20**.
- Spearow has shorter wings and a feisty, territorial disposition. Fearow is physically larger, with a long beak and stronger aerial hunting capacity.
- In anime episode 1, Spearow's flock pursues Ash and Pikachu. The same Spearow subsequently returns as Fearow in “Pallet Party Panic!” and is opposed by Ash’s newly evolved Pidgeot. This makes a *real cross-line rivalry with Pidgey/Pidgeotto/Pidgeot*, and should not be represented as merely generic birds in battle.

## Existing Workout Dex move constraints
- Pidgeotto’s **Agility** uses a lateral shuffle into short acceleration. Fearow's **Agility** must use that exact exercise family and nearly identical camera angle.
- Pidgey **Tackle** uses a light sled push; the birds do not need a Tackle move just for symmetry.
- Butterfree’s **Air Slash** and the Pidgey family's Wing Attack emphasize a high-to-low cable sweep. Spearow/Fearow Aerial Ace may use a closely related wing-path exercise with different game move names.
- Neither proposed unordered four-move set duplicates any of #001–020; the two proposed combinations are distinct from each other.

## Training dosage and rationale
- **Spearow Flock Fighter:** Beginner+, ~40–50 minutes, 16 working sets; deliberately controlled cue acceleration, band press, cable rhythm and cable sweep plus goblet squat, RDL and chest-supported row.
- **Fearow Aerial Enforcer:** Intermediate–Advanced, ~60–70 minutes, 21 working sets; med-ball scoop toss while fresh, landmine forward press, Pidgeotto-matched agility, deadlift, cable sweep, row and reverse lunge.
- Real speed/strength progression, not simply more repetitions; no max sprinting, neck-driven drills or actual pecking.
- Four official moves, seven actual exercises, warm-up/cool-down, scalable regressions and three Pokémon/Trainer/pre-filming feasibility tests each.
